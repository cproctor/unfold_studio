# Theme development

I want to develop a new front-end theme for Unfold Studio. In addition to generally 
updating the site's look and making it consistent across the site 
(numerous devs started developing themes which are inconsistently applied), the look and 
feel of the site is an important learning mechanism. 

Use the /frontend-design skill for this task. 

Therefore I want to design the site theme in a principled way, hoping that the site's 
ethos and vibe encourages users to show up in certain ways, and to participate in certain 
ways. For theorizing this, I want to use a conjecture map (use the /conjecture-mapping skill). 
Read the references in the `references` directory, which articulate the high-level goals and conjectures
for this project. (Also read https://chrisproctor.net/dissertation for the same purpose.)

Then create a conjecture map in theme_conjectures.v0.md, containing a mermaid 
conjecture map (formatted like the conjectures shortcode in ~/Repos/computationalliteracies/site/content/projects/interactive_storytelling/conjectures.md)
as well as explanatory text focusing on design features (embodiment, in the language 
of conjecture mapping) specific to the theme/style/UI/ethos/vibe of the site. This should connect specific
design elements to mediating processes which might follow from them. Some examples, which should be 
included, are listed in the design conjectures section below. For the proposed embodiments, we are not 
currently focused on creating polished products--a high-level description of what we'll do 
(e.g. "retro-style icons") in the conjecture map, and create a directory called examples containing wireframes, 
prototypes, and examples from precedents. Pull these into the main doc as examples of the embodiment we will
enact. 

We are not implementing any theming right now; we are just designing themes. I will give feedback after you create
the first theme_conjectures proposal.

## Design conjectures

- The whole site will feel "retro", with a fixed-width font used in most places and a mostly monochrome color scheme.
  - For some users, this may evoke a "Web 1.0" era in which interaction online was 
    more authentic and positive. 
  - The core mechanism of the site is reading and writing stories, with the ability to 
    see how they work (permeable media). The site's look and feel should subtly reinforce
    its hackability--instead of presenting a perfect, smooth, polished surface which is 
    impenetrable (e.g. Apple's design), it should feel like there is not too much distance
    between what you see and how it is implemented. 
  - The feel will connote computer science, but will have a very different emphasis than most CS
    (it's about writing, about sharing feelings and experiences, and connecting with people); this 
    could help people to build positive CS identities, or to resist exclusive stereotypes about CS.
- The site will have playful flourishes in aspects such as icons, pilcrows, etc. Drawing from examples such 
  as Susan Kare's original mac icons, icons from the original Legend of Zelda, and, more recently, Undertale. 
  - This will connote a valuing of craft, suggesting that this is a place where we care about 
    what we make. It will feel human, not corporate. 
- The site will toggle between immersion and interactivity (see dissertation), at times inviting people to get
  lost in stories, and at other times inviting them to pay attention to the interface boundaries.
- The site will emphasize relationships between readers and writers.
